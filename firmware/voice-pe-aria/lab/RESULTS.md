## How to read the measures (simulated lab estimates)

- **Catch rate (true wake, TPR):** share of 144 held-out synthetic recordings of the candidate
  (24 voices × careful/intended/casual forms) that woke the model, pooled over 18 conditions
  (volume, 1/2/4 m room, bed facing away, TV on, another talker, HVAC/music/background, weak onset,
  shortened ending, run-together, simulated older/raspy/denture speech).
- **Wrong-word wakes (confusion rate):** share of synthetic recordings of other words that wrongly
  woke the model: sound-alikes (area, Maria, Krista, Sylvia…), launcher-only and senior-living
  vocabulary, everyday sentences and the supplemental confusion list. Wakes on other candidates'
  phrases are reported separately (cross-triggers).
- **Simulated false wakes per hour (ambient FA/h):** wakes counted over about 5.6 h of ambient audio
  (2.8 h TV-dialogue proxy, 1 h music, 0.33 h room background, 1 h HVAC, 0.5 h silence), 2 s
  refractory. One wake ≈ 0.18/h, so 0.355/h means two wakes in 5.6 h. This is a lab estimate, not
  an established real-room hourly rate.
- Each model is compared at its **matched cutoff**: the lowest threshold where that simulated
  ambient rate is ≤ 0.5/h. A model that can't get there scores 0 on false wakes.
- Score = catch rate 30%, wrong-word wakes 30%, simulated older resident 20%, memorability 10%,
  natural identity 10% (the last two are human judgements, shown raw).
- Ties are broken by candidate id. Five finalists = the best candidate of each distinct base word,
  so launcher forms and pronunciation variants of one word cannot crowd a different word off the
  list (passed over this time: okay aria, behind hey aria).

| Per-clip noise/augmentation seed used Python `hash()`, which is salted per process: each model heard different noise segments on the shared clips | seed = `SEED + crc32(relative path \| condition)`; all 26 evaluations + cross-checks re-run, no retraining | see below |

**Evaluation fairness, after the fix:** all 26 evaluations use one identical negative job set
(same files × conditions, digest `595acae420dd`) and one identical set of ambient streams; the same
clip and condition produce byte-identical mixed audio in two separate processes (verified). Every
evaluation's model hash matches its trained model.

**Ranking before vs after all corrections:** scores moved by at most 0.035, except Zaria (−0.305).

clip was mixed with varied between models. The pre-correction ranking is kept in

## Findings

1. **No candidate is resident-ready on synthetic evidence.** Best catch rate 75%; with the TV on, 51%
   or lower for every model. Real voices on the real Voice PE microphones may do better or worse.
2. **A launcher helps (measured).** In 9 of 11 bare-vs-launcher pairs the launcher form caught more
   (kookaburra 34% → 75%, callista 11% → 58%, krysta 21% → 64%, velora 32% → 55%, aria 29% → 44%).
   Exceptions: sivia SEE-vee-uh (bare 56% vs 47%, but bare fails the ambient limit) and pomona
   (27% vs 25%). Most bare words cannot reach the simulated ambient limit at all.
3. **The text screen over-penalises launcher phrases with names.** Hey Callista and Hey Krysta failed
   it on name collisions but measured 0.22% and 0.18% wrong-word wakes. It remains a useful filter
   for single words.
4. **"Aria" stays risky.** Bare aria (1.97%) and hey aria (1.63%) are among the four highest
   wrong-word rates in the lab, consistent with the Room 214 "area" false wakes.
5. **Pronunciation variants are real differences.** Callista kuh-LISS-tuh vs CALL-iss-tuh audio
   differs significantly (Wilcoxon p = 4e-5). Sivia SIV-ee-uh vs SEE-vee-uh was not significant
   (p = 0.071), but the trained models behave differently (bare 19% vs 56%), so they stay separate.
